The Cost of Stale Telemetry in Traffic Engineering: Learned Policies, Re-Tuned Heuristics and ECMP

Draft v4 (2026-10-14), not yet submitted: venue, authors and the public repository URL are placeholders, and the manual literature pass (Google Scholar, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore) is not complete. All numbers come from scripts in experiments/; raw results are in experiments/final/. Protocol deviations are tabulated in docs/eval_protocol.md; claims and their status are in Section 8.

Abstract

Which traffic-engineering controller should an operator run when the telemetry it relies on is stale or noisy? We compare ECMP, a reactive heuristic re-tuned for each telemetry condition (five independent tuning runs, 80 trials each), and a learned split-ratio policy trained only on small synthetic graphs, under one impairment model shared by a fluid simulator and an emulated SDN, on 24 held-out Topology Zoo graphs and on replayed SNDlib traces. The answer depends on conditions. With clean or moderately impaired telemetry the re-tuned heuristic matches or beats the learned policy on average (Zoo and real traces: heuristic ahead or tied under ideal, rich and SNMP-like telemetry). Under heavily degraded telemetry (delay, noise and drops together) the learned policy is ahead on average (Zoo +0.033 dScore, 95% interval [+0.018, +0.049], 0 losses in 24 graphs; all 9 real-trace cells), and its mean p99 regret is lower than the heuristic's in every telemetry profile (0.017-0.019 vs 0.036-0.092), although per graph the learned policy has the lower tail on only 9-13 of 24 graphs, so the tail advantage comes from a minority of graphs where the heuristic does badly. In a controlled delay x polling grid the heuristic's gain declines with both delay and polling period while the learned policy's does not, with a network-dependent magnitude (clear on GEANT, unresolved on Abilene); there is no universal delay threshold. Ablations show that model-based fluid features matter and graph message passing does not (a per-link MLP does as well). Single heuristic tuning runs can change a comparison as much as the effects reported. Telemetry faults are modelled rather than measured and traces are scaled to target loads, so the results are not a validation in a production network.

1 Introduction

Telemetry quality is a missing dimension in the evaluation of learned traffic engineering. Learned TE policies are typically trained and tested with perfect inputs; operational controllers poll counters at fixed periods and act on stale, noisy and incomplete views. Heuristics tuned to the latest counters can chase stale information, and a learned policy trained on clean inputs may misbehave. Prior work shows that neural routers degrade under realistic delay (Section 2), so we do not claim that observation. We ask a narrower, operational question:

For a given network and telemetry quality, which of ECMP, a tuned reactive heuristic, or a learned policy should an operator run, and what does each cost in tail risk and in controller activity?

We contribute (i) an impairment model shared by simulator and controller and a like-for-like comparison against a heuristic re-tuned for every telemetry profile; (ii) evidence on held-out Topology Zoo graphs and replayed real traces that the ranking depends on telemetry quality, with the conditions under which it does not (clean telemetry, overload, per-network training data); (iii) an ablation and baseline study that identifies what carries the result; (iv) an empirical evaluation of an ECMP-fallback guard and of a rate limiter; and (v) an open audit tool that applies these measurements to a user's network. Negative results (LP-teacher distillation, PPO fine-tuning) are reported.

2 Related work

Learned TE. DOTE (Perry et al., NSDI 2023) trains a network on recent demand matrices to output split ratios over candidate paths by directly minimising maximum link utilisation; our training method is of this family, and we add a differentiable fluid model with a packet-loss term and training under impaired observations. FIGRET (Liu et al., SIGCOMM 2024) adds burst robustness to this family; FlexDATE (Ye et al., IEEE/ACM ToN 2023) and its predecessor DATE (IWQoS 2021) choose critical flows with RL and reroute them by LP, with INT/SNMP telemetry assumed timely; Teal (Xu et al., SIGCOMM 2023) and CFR-RL (Zhang et al., IEEE JSAC 2020) target scale or rerouting. None of these treats telemetry staleness, noise or missing counters as an experimental variable (full texts read; DOTE tests demand noise up to +-35% only; Teal's delay is solver latency). Staleness. Roracle (Ye et al., ICNP 2023) is the closest in spirit: it shows that LP-optimal routing computed on the current matrix degrades as it ages (about 20% in MLU over a five-step horizon) and that a learned lookahead resists this; the staleness there is routing-computation delay and traffic drift, there is no telemetry-impairment sweep and no tuned reactive baseline, and it evaluates on Abilene, GEANT and Brain among others. LOGGIA (Boltres et al., TMLR 2026, arXiv 2604.02927) models communication and inference delay for neural link-weight routing in a packet-level simulator and reports that neural baselines stop beating static shortest-path routing under delay; it compares with default shortest-path protocols, studies one impairment axis and has no safety mechanism. EnvelopeTC (Jia et al., IEEE JCC 2026) bounds agent actions and disables reroutes after a staleness limit in a hierarchical data-centre setting. RedTE (Gui et al., SIGCOMM 2024) removes centralised control-loop latency by moving decisions to routers. Safe fallback. Training Wheels (Mao et al., NeurIPS 2019) falls back to a simple policy in unsafe states; our guard applies this idea, and FIGRET's hedging and Safe Load Balancing (arXiv 2410.16846) pursue related goals by different means. Novelty. We do not claim to be first to observe that stale inputs hurt (LOGGIA, Roracle, Teal all touch staleness). What we add is an empirical result: in a controlled impairment sweep that holds all other factors fixed and compares against a heuristic re-tuned per condition, telemetry delay and polling period, but not noise or missing counters, decide whether a reactive heuristic or a learned policy is the better choice, on real topologies and traces. In our literature search (a few query variants plus full-text reading of the papers above) we found no paper that sweeps polling period, delay, noise, missing counters and install latency together for centralised split-ratio TE; this is a search result, not proof of absence, and a Scholar check on "measurement staleness SDN TE" should precede submission (docs/related_work.md).

3 Setting

Network and traffic. A topology with link capacities and k = 4 candidate paths per demand; the policy outputs split ratios each interval. Traffic is synthetic (gravity base, diurnal cycle, bursts, hotspots, shifts, flash crowds) or a replayed SNDlib trace (Abilene, 5-min, 48,096 matrices; GEANT, 15-min, 11,460; Germany17 and Germany50, 5-min, 288 each; Brain, 1-min, 9,723, restricted to its 600 heaviest demands = 90% of sampled traffic). Each (network, load) pair uses one scale factor so that the median ECMP max-link-utilisation over the trace equals 0.6, 0.8 or 1.0. This is real-trace evaluation under controlled load scaling: temporal and spatial structure are real, absolute load is not. The scale factors are large and differ by network (experiments/final/table_scale_factors.md): to reach a median ECMP max-utilisation of 0.8 the Abilene and Brain traces are multiplied by about 9 and 23, GEANT by about 4, while the Germany17 and Germany50 traces are multiplied by about 0.02 (their native demands exceed the published module capacities by a factor of about 30-40, so capacity units in those files are not meaningful on their own). Some traces contain extreme intervals: the largest GEANT interval is about 12,000 times its median, so after scaling it has a max-utilisation near 10^4; Abilene's maximum is about 17 times its median. Such intervals dominate pooled tail statistics and are the likely source of the 'overloaded windows' of Section 6.4.

Telemetry impairment. The controller observes utilisation and demands through a channel with polling period P (intervals), delay d, multiplicative noise sigma and per-counter drop probability p; installing rules takes L intervals. Profiles: ideal (1,0,0,0), rich (1,1,0.03,0), SNMP-like (5,1,0.05,0.02), degraded (5,3,0.2,0.1). The same channel is applied to real OpenFlow counters in the emulated controller.

Metrics. We report separately: gain (reduction of mean max-link-utilisation vs ECMP), fluid packet loss, tail regret (p99 of per-interval max-util minus ECMP's; positive = worse than ECMP) and flow-update rate. The composite score (max-util ratio + 5 x loss, paired difference to ECMP, "dScore") is a convenience; conclusions do not depend on the weight (Section 6.1). Unit of analysis: for Zoo results, the graph (paired across methods; seeds and training seeds are averaged within a graph and reported as spread); for real traces, the (network, load) cell with independent trace windows inside it; windows within one day-long trace overlap and are not treated as independent.

4 Methods

Baselines. ECMP. Tuned heuristic: EWMA-smoothed bottleneck soft-min over candidate paths with hysteresis, damped updates and age-based shrinkage of stale readings; five parameters tuned per telemetry profile by a 40-trial random search on four synthetic graphs disjoint from training and test graphs (it therefore knows the telemetry profile, a favourable baseline). Static LP: LP-optimal splits for the mean traffic matrix of the training part of a trace, no telemetry. DOTE-style: an MLP on the last three impaired demand estimates, trained per network on the first 70% of its trace (ideal-telemetry and degraded-telemetry variants) with our differentiable loss; evaluated on the last 30%. LP oracle: perfect information, graphs with n <= 25 only; a reference bound, not a deployable method.

Learned policy. A graph network on the link graph with path pooling (Section 6.3 shows the message passing is not what matters; a per-link MLP with the same inputs does as well or better). Inputs: impaired utilisation and demand history (H = 3), telemetry age, and model-based fluid features (ECMP-predicted utilisation, utilisation predicted for the current configuration, per-path what-if bottleneck). Training is direct optimisation through a differentiable fluid model: the policy sees the impaired observation, the loss uses the true next demand, smoothmax(util)/ecmp_mu + 5 loss + 0.1 churn. Training graphs are 40 synthetic BA/ER/geometric graphs with 10-20 nodes; no Zoo or SNDlib graph is used. Variants: randomised-telemetry training ("ours") and ideal-only training; four seeds each. Fine-tuning: the pre-trained model is trained for 6 iterations (~3-5 minutes on CPU) on rollouts over the first 70% of a network's trace with randomised telemetry and evaluated on the last 30%.

Guard. L1: stale or missing telemetry, malformed action, deadline. L2: fluid-model check of the proposal against ECMP on the estimated state, rejecting it if predicted max-util exceeds ECMP's by more than a margin eps. L3: runtime monitor with backoff. The headline guard is L2 only with eps = -0.06, chosen on validation graphs. Property claimed: none formal. The guard is an empirically evaluated runtime filter; we report its fallback rate, false-rejection rate, the share of harmful proposals caught and the regret before and after (Section 6.5).

Rate limiter. A demand's installed split is kept unless the proposal moves it by more than a threshold (half the L1 distance); the number of updates per interval is the flow/group modifications a controller would send.

Audit tool. stale-but-safe audit takes a topology, optional traffic and telemetry parameters and reports best-case headroom (LP oracle where n <= 25), gain with a 95% bootstrap interval over trace windows and tail regret for each method under the user's telemetry, a polling sweep, and one of three outcomes: stay on ECMP, use a named method, or insufficient evidence (too few windows, interval straddling the 5% threshold, or gain concentrated in one window). "ECMP is enough" is declared only when the upper end of the 95% interval of the best method's gain under perfect telemetry is below a prespecified 5% max-utilisation tolerance, never from a non-significant difference alone; the 5% value is a practical choice that operators can change, not a derived constant. It flags telemetry values outside the evaluated range. Its output is an offline estimate, not a deployment recommendation.

5 Protocol

Topology Zoo: 263 graphs split into train/validation/test (configs/splits.yaml); we report the 24-graph test_subset, 5 evaluation seeds, horizon 100, load 0.8; paired bootstrap 95% CI, IQM and Holm-corrected Wilcoxon over graphs. Real traces: four networks with headroom plus Brain, loads {0.6, 0.8, 1.0}, four profiles; two independent evaluation runs (4 windows, then 10 fresh windows per cell, 12 for Brain at load 0.8). Tuning and model selection never use test graphs or windows. Interval methodology: Zoo intervals are paired bootstrap over graphs on seed-averaged scores, so they capture graph variability but not training-seed variability (reported separately as seed SD, 0.001-0.002; the GNN-vs-MLP and fluid-feature comparisons use four matched training seeds each, averaged per graph before resampling). Real-trace intervals are paired bootstrap over evaluation windows for one trained model and one trace, so they do not generalise to other days or networks. The 5% ECMP tolerance is applied identically everywhere: "ECMP is enough" requires the interval's upper end to be below 5%; a lower end below 5% gives "insufficient evidence". Deviations from the planned protocol (4 instead of 10 training seeds, 5-12 instead of 20 evaluation seeds, single-seed ablations, post-hoc baselines and analyses) are listed in docs/eval_protocol.md.

Two conclusions. First, a heuristic whose parameters are frozen collapses with delay (to about zero at d >= 5), but this is largely an artefact of mis-tuning: re-tuned at each delay it keeps most of its gain. Second, a re-tuned heuristic matches the learned policy at small delay (d = 3: Abilene 34.7 vs 34.2, GEANT 43.6 vs 42.5, Zoo 3.2 vs 4.3) and is behind at d = 5 and d = 10 in this table (Abilene 23.2 and 21.0 vs about 34.6; GEANT 23.2 and 24.9 vs about 42). The learned policy's gain does not depend on d. The interaction grid below, with repeated tuning, shows the boundary depends on polling period and network. The re-tuning budget is modest (40 trials, synthetic graphs, other impairments ideal), so a stronger tuned baseline might narrow the gap; this is not tested. The per-profile comparisons of Sections 6.1-6.2 use heuristics re-tuned for each profile, so they are not affected by the frozen-parameter artefact.

6.2b Sensitivity to the load-scaling rule

Scaling each real trace so that the mean or the 95th percentile (instead of the median) of the ECMP max-utilisation equals 0.8 (table_scaling.md; Abilene, GEANT, Germany17; ideal and degraded telemetry; 6 windows; 18 cells): learned ahead in 12, tuned ahead in 4 (clean telemetry on Abilene and Germany17, and Abilene at the 95th percentile with degraded telemetry), 2 ties; under degraded telemetry the learned policy is ahead in 8 of 9 cells. The conclusion does not depend on the scaling rule; the absolute numbers do.

6.3 What carries the result: baselines and ablations

Held-out windows (last 30% of the trace), gain % (experiments/final/table_ft.md; seed-0 GNN):

Abilene @0.8 tuned static LP DOTE-style (degraded-trained) GNN zero-shot GNN fine-tuned
ideal 33.6 34.5 49.1 23.7 46.2
SNMP 27.7 34.5 49.1 22.4 41.3
degraded 23.5 34.5 48.9 22.5 39.3

On GEANT the DOTE-style model reaches about 18% in every profile, the zero-shot policy 15-18%, the fine-tuned policy 16-19% and static LP -38.6% (traffic is not stationary). Hence: (a) with per-network history a DOTE-style model beats the zero-shot GNN, and fine-tuning recovers most of the difference on Abilene but little on GEANT; (b) robustness to telemetry is a property of any policy that does not rely on fresh counters, not specific to our GNN; (c) what the zero-shot policy offers is no need for per-network training data. Ablations (24 Zoo graphs; table_ablations.md, one seed, and table_gnn_vs_mlp.md, four training seeds each for the full model, the per-link MLP and the no-fluid-feature model): removing history costs +0.003 at ideal telemetry only; removing the age input is ~0. Matched-seed GNN vs MLP: the per-link MLP (no message passing) has mean dScore -0.068 (ideal) / -0.063 (degraded) against -0.065 / -0.061 for the full graph model; seed-averaged paired difference -0.0031 [-0.0054, -0.0011] and -0.0020 [-0.0040, -0.0002], the MLP better on 14 of 24 graphs in both profiles. Its p99 regret is slightly worse under degraded telemetry (0.022 vs 0.019). So message passing does not help; the effect sizes are small and we claim only "no benefit", not that MLPs are better. Fluid-model features: removing them costs +0.0082 [+0.0048, +0.0121] (ideal) and +0.0144 [+0.0085, +0.0206] (degraded) and worsens p99 regret (0.018 to 0.029 degraded); the full model is better on 21-22 of 24 graphs. The model-based features, not the graph architecture, carry the result. Training with randomised telemetry versus ideal-only: the mean differences are within seed noise (~0.002), but the randomised model has a lower p99 regret under degraded telemetry (0.019 vs 0.027 over four seeds).

6.4 Where it fails: overload

Real-trace windows in which ECMP's own mean max-utilisation exceeds 2 (traffic surges far above the requested load) lie outside the training range (loads 0.5-1.1). On Abilene at load 0.8 over 30 windows: in 27 typical windows the learned policy has median gain 36.5% and is never worse than ECMP, while the tuned heuristic has median 31.5% (worst window -50.8% under degraded telemetry); in 3 overloaded windows the tuned heuristic gains 45-49% and the learned policy 9-21% (worse than ECMP in one of three; worst case +3 to +7 in absolute max-util). The guard lifts the learned policy to 20-27% in those windows (table_overload.md). Tail statistics pooled over windows are therefore dominated by a few surges. Repeated with the re-tuned heuristic (table_overload_v2.md, same 30 windows): typical windows, ideal telemetry, heuristic median gain 41.1% (worst 8.6%) vs learned 36.5% (worst 2.1%); degraded telemetry, heuristic 28.6% (worst -21.5%) vs learned 35.3% (worst +1.4%, never below ECMP); overloaded windows (3), heuristic 49.0% (ideal) and 42.5% (degraded) vs learned 9.1% and 20.8%, lifted by the guard to 19.9% and 26.8%. The qualitative picture is unchanged: the heuristic is better on average with ideal telemetry and in overloaded windows, the learned policy is better and safer under degraded telemetry.

6.5 Guard

Definitions (per interval, using the true demand, simulator only): beneficial proposal = true max-util below 0.99 x ECMP's, harmful = above 1.01 x; false rejection = beneficial proposals among fallbacks; harm caught = harmful proposals replaced. Zoo graphs (10 graphs with n <= 25, table_guard_audit.md), headline guard (L2 only, eps = -0.06):

scenario fallback false rejection harm caught gain kept p99 regret raw -> guarded
ideal 71% 12% 43% 83% +0.050 -> +0.019
degraded 72% 14% 22% 76% +0.065 -> +0.057
link failure, degraded 80% 13% 90% 91% +0.148 -> +0.021
demand shift, degraded 73% 18% 53% 74% +0.084 -> +0.058

On real Abilene and GEANT traces (load 0.8, three typical windows each) the guard fires in only 0-2% of intervals because the learned policy's proposals are essentially never harmful there (0.0% of accepted intervals harmful; raw p99 regret already <= 0); its value on real traces appears in overloaded windows (Section 6.4) and for the tuned heuristic. On the 24 test graphs the per-graph p99 regret falls from 0.019 to 0.008 at degraded telemetry (average of per-graph p99), while the pooled p99 above falls only from +0.065 to +0.057: the guard cuts the typical tail but misses harmful proposals concentrated in a few graphs. The guard is most effective under link failures. It falls back often (71-80%) yet keeps 74-91% of the gain because fallback occurs mostly in low-gain intervals. For the tuned heuristic under degraded telemetry the guard improves the gain by 2.3 points on real traces and lowers its p99 regret from 0.198 to 0.074; the validation-chosen guard barely affects the learned policy on real traces (0.0-0.8 points).

The guard roughly halves the p99 regret of both policies (learned 0.017-0.019 to 0.008-0.010; heuristic 0.026-0.093 to 0.008-0.031) and its fallback rate is 78-84%. Its cost in average dScore is about +0.011 for the learned policy in every profile and +0.015 to +0.021 for the heuristic at ideal, rich and SNMP telemetry, but essentially nothing for the heuristic under degraded telemetry (-0.040 to -0.042), where it lowers p99 regret from 0.093 to 0.030. With the guard, the learned policy and the re-tuned heuristic have similar p99 regret at ideal and rich telemetry (0.008 vs 0.008, 0.009 vs 0.019) and the learned policy is lower elsewhere. The guard is therefore a tail-risk trade against mean performance for either policy, not a property that favours the learned one.

6.6 Operational cost and emulation

Without a rate limiter the learned policy updates 91% of demands every interval in simulation (Zoo, ideal telemetry); a 5% threshold cuts this to 8% and a 10% threshold to 2.4% with unchanged gain (3.1%) (table_ratelimit.md); the tuned heuristic already updates about 2.5% of demands. On the real traces (table_ratelimit_real.md, degraded telemetry) the learned policy updates 98.5% (Abilene) and 100% (GEANT) of demands per interval without a limiter; thresholds of 5%, 10% and 20% cut this to 5.1/1.7/0.9% (Abilene) and 6.3/1.9/0.8% (GEANT) while the gain stays or rises (Abilene 26.4 -> 27.6/32.4/36.8%, GEANT 37.9 -> 38.2/38.5/37.7%; thresholds of 0.5 lose gain). The threshold also damps oscillation, so the update-rate cost is largely avoidable in simulation.

Emulation: Mininet with Open vSwitch and a Ryu controller reading real OpenFlow counters, 24 flows, four scenarios (three traffic seeds on a 10-node graph, one 12-node random graph), six policy/telemetry runs each plus a run with a 20% update threshold (table_emu2.md, controller with the fail-safe fixes, 28 runs). Simulator and emulator agree: across the 24 runs at the default 5% threshold the correlation between emulated and simulated mean max-utilisation is 0.99, the mean absolute difference 0.017 and the bias +0.010; per-run time-series correlation is 0.83-0.98; the policy ranking agrees (Kendall tau 0.47-0.73 per scenario). Means over the four scenarios (emulated max-util / flow-mods per minute / control loop):

policy [telemetry] max-util flow-mods/min loop ms

The re-tuned heuristic is much better than the original one under SNMP telemetry (0.772 vs 0.798, close to the learned policy's 0.763) but reprograms about 140 flows per minute instead of 30, so the learned policy reprograms about two times more flows than the re-tuned heuristic (270-332 vs 139), not ten times. Under degraded telemetry the learned policy is ahead (0.772 vs 0.806, loss 0.0080 vs 0.0095) in all four scenarios (by only 0.001 in emu10_s0); under SNMP the two are level on average (per scenario the learned policy is lower in three and higher in one). Cost grows roughly with links x demands, which is fine for minute-scale control intervals but not for sub-second loops on networks above about 50 nodes with all-pairs demands.

6.7 Audit tool

Run on four real networks with degraded telemetry (polling every 5 intervals, delay 3, noise 0.2, loss 0.1; 6 trace windows; reports/*.html), using the re-tuned heuristic. Abilene: use the tuned heuristic (30.6%, 95% interval [27.2, 34.1]; best-case headroom 56.5%), not distinguishable from the learned policy or its guarded version on these six windows; GEANT: use the learned policy (37.5% [23.1, 51.0]), with a warning that the heuristic is worse than ECMP in 7% of intervals and that no method keeps p99 regret under 0.02; Germany50: use the learned policy (33.9% [30.7, 36.8]), not distinguishable from its guarded version; Brain: stay on ECMP, flagged provisional because six windows cannot rule out the rare surge window in which the learned policy gained 21%. With the original heuristic the Abilene and GEANT recommendations were the opposite (learned and guarded heuristic respectively; reports/v1_original_heuristic/): with six windows and a close call, the recommendation depends on the heuristic's tuning, which is the paper's own point and a reason to run the tool on more windows. The tool therefore reports intervals and ties, not only a winner, and says when the sample is too small. It remains an offline estimate; its verdicts have not been validated against any operator decision.

7 Negative results

LP-teacher distillation (DAgger) produced a weak student (6.4% gain vs 15.0% for the tuned heuristic on training graphs); PPO fine-tuning did not change it; direct optimisation through the fluid model fixed this. Randomised-telemetry training did not improve mean performance over ideal-only training. A per-link MLP did as well as the GNN, with four matched seeds each (Section 6.3). Static LP routing is excellent on a stationary network (Abilene) and disastrous on a non-stationary one (GEANT).

8 Limitations and status of claims

  • Fluid model: no TCP, queueing or packet dynamics. The guard offers no formal guarantee; it is an empirically evaluated filter and misses most harmful proposals under degraded telemetry on Zoo graphs.
  • Real traffic but scaled to a target load by factors from 0.02 to 23 (Section 3); trace capacities for Germany17/50 are not meaningful in absolute terms; some traces contain extreme outlier intervals. The conclusion is insensitive to the scaling rule (6.2b), the absolute numbers are not. Capacities are the networks' published module sizes where available.
  • Telemetry faults are modelled, not measured on a deployed network.
  • Four training seeds (not ten); ablations, DOTE-style and fine-tuned models have one seed; evaluation seeds 4-12 (not 20).
  • DOTE-style and fine-tuned models need per-network history; only Abilene and GEANT have enough.
  • Germany17 and Germany50 have one day of data; their windows overlap.
  • Out-of-range overload hurts the zero-shot policy (6.4).
  • No comparison with Teal or other published systems (Teal's full text was read: it optimises total flow or MLU against LP/NCFlow/POP baselines with accurate demand inputs and treats staleness only as solver run time, so it is not a like-for-like baseline for telemetry impairments); DOTE-style is our re-implementation of the idea, not the authors' code.
  • The heuristic's tuning budget is 80 trials per tuner seed on four synthetic graphs; spread over five tuner seeds is up to ~0.02 dScore on Zoo and ~10 points of gain in the delay grid, so single-run comparisons are fragile. The overload (6.4), loss-weight and emulation results use the original 40-trial heuristic and were not repeated. The in-domain tuning of the heuristic on a network's own history (which the learned policy's fine-tuning variants receive) was not tested.
  • Not claimed: a general performance win; being first to show degradation under delay (LOGGIA); production readiness.
